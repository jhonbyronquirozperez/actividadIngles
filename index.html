My Jungle Book: The Olympics of My Life

Jhon Byron Quiroz - Full-Stack Developer

01

My Player Profile

"Who Am I in the Jungle?"

Hi, I'm Jhon Byron Quiroz, a full-stack developer. I love web and mobile development because I enjoy turning ideas into real, useful products. I can build complete applications with React, PHP, WordPress, and Node. My biggest challenge is managing my time: I work, study, learn English, and take care of my home, so I sometimes have to set priorities. Right now, I'm working on finishing my degree.

02

My Pororoca: My Life Challenge

"What's the big wave I must face?"

My biggest challenge is finishing my degree and finding a stable job. Artificial intelligence is changing the tech industry, and I have been affected by it. It is hard because the world is still discovering this change, and I must accept it. My challenge is like a pororoca: a huge wave that could push me down, but it might also carry me forward if I learn to surf it. If I stop trying, I could lose my chance.

03

Rules of My Game

"What rules help me reach my goals?"

  1. I must tell the truth, even when it is difficult.
  2. I mustn't lie to myself about my progress.
  3. I have to finish my degree, step by step.
  4. I need to plan my week so I don't lose track of my priorities.
  5. I should study English every day, even for a few minutes.
  6. I shouldn't accept more work than I can handle at the same time.
  7. I ought to rest when I feel tired, so I can keep going.
  8. I must keep looking for new opportunities every day.
  9. I mustn't give up on my goals because of the difficult moments.
  10. I should ask for help when I need it.
04

Training and Progress

"How have I changed during my race?"

When I started learning English, I could hardly follow a conversation. Today I can watch some series, understand part of the actions and expressions, and enjoy songs more than before. I still don’t understand everything, but I catch more words every week. I have also learned to adapt English phrases to my own modern way of speaking, and I can make jokes with them. My studies have been harder since artificial intelligence arrived, and sometimes I feel slower than before. Even so, I have kept learning on my own. Looking back, I am more patient and more confident, and my race is not finished yet.

05

Advice for Future Athletes

"What can others learn from my story?"

To the new students and future athletes: you will face waves that are bigger than you expect. Don’t pretend everything is fine. Tell the truth to yourself, even when it hurts. You should set priorities, but you shouldn’t carry everything alone. Rest when you need to, study a little every day, and ask for help when the wave is too strong. You ought to keep going, one step at a time, because the race is still yours.

06

Final Reflection: My Olympic Values

"What have I learned from my race?"

My race has taught me that the truth is my most important value. In a world full of lies, I try to be honest with myself about my progress and my difficulties. I have also learned that I can keep learning English on my own, and that every small step counts. My next goal is to finish my degree and find a stable job, even though artificial intelligence has changed the rules of the game. I will keep learning English, one series, one song, and one conversation at a time.
